Character Architecture
Build each major character in layers, moving between outside-in and inside-out:

The Two-Layer Rule (all three books converge here)
Characterization (the mask): All observable traits — age, speech, job, clothing, personality, the Big Five spectra. Its three jobs: credibility, originality, intrigue.
True Character (the core): Revealed only through choices under pressure. Design a dilemma — a choice between two irreconcilable goods or two evils.
The Four Selves (depth map)
Core Self — stable identity, secrets, willpower
Personal Selves — intimacy variants (family, lovers, friends)
Social Selves — power/role variants (job, public persona)
Hidden Self — subconscious drives, taboo desires
Dimensions = Contradictions
Give the protagonist consistent contradictions (Odysseus: truthful/deceitful; Tony Soprano: guilt-ridden/sociopath). Aim for the protagonist to carry the most dimensions in the cast. Use the six contradiction types (characterization vs. true character, conscious vs. subconscious desire, etc.).

The Desire Complex (this is the bridge to plot AND dialogue)
For each major character define:

Object of Desire — what they consciously pursue
Super-Intention — the deeper hunger driving them
Unconscious Desire — ideally contradicting the conscious one
Motivation — the "why" (never a lazy trauma-excuse)
Self-Story & M.O. — the story they tell themselves and the tactics they habitually use
