Phase 6 — The Dialogue Pass
Now, and only now, write the words — flowing outward from everything above.

The Three Concentric Spheres (per line)
The Said — surface words
The Unsaid — deliberately withheld thoughts (readable subtext)
The Unsayable — subconscious drives, expressed only at breaking points
The Six Dialogue Tasks (every line, simultaneously)
Takes an inner action → intensifies toward the turning point → conveys exposition → characterizes → drives momentum → rings true.

Craft techniques
Exposition as ammunition: Facts emerge as weapons in conflict, never info-dumps.
The trialogue: Route conflict through a "third thing" (wine in Sideways, a movie in Out of Sight) to keep truth in the subtext.
Character-specific voice: Pull vocabulary from the dossier's Voice DNA — nouns/verbs reveal knowledge; modifiers reveal emotion; syntax reveals temperament. Fear shortens lines; rationality lengthens them.
Line design: Suspense sentence (punchline last, for tension), cumulative (core first, conversational), balanced. Time the core word for the cue.
Economy & silence: Omit needless words; a pause or a wordless gesture is the ultimate economy.
Credibility flaws

 Empty talk (no subtext, exists only to explain)
 Overly emotive talk (drama exceeds authentic feeling)
 Overly knowing talk (impossible self-awareness / author's mouthpiece)
 Excuses mistaken for motivation (trauma-as-shorthand)
 Melodrama (overexpression from under-motivation)
Content flaws

 On-the-nose writing (character states exactly what they feel) → mask it; add a trialogue
 Monologue fallacy (long unbroken speech) → interlace with action/reaction
 Duelogue collapse (raw face-to-face shouting) → introduce a third thing
Design flaws

 Repetitious beats (same value charge repeated)
 Blurred meaning (generic words + adjective bloat)
 Mistimed core words/cues
 Misshapen scene (no turning point / anticlimax)
 The paraphrasing trap: if rewording doesn't fix it, the problem is the scene design — go back to Phase 5, not the thesaurus
The Golden Thread That Ties It All Together
If you remember nothing else, the combined system reduces to five linked laws:

Desire drives everything — no desire, no scene, no line.
Pressure reveals truth — characterization is the mask; choice under pressure is the character.
Every unit must turn a value — beat, scene, sequence, act, story.
Subtext over text — what's said hides what's meant; the audience decodes.
Write inside-out — dialogue is the last step of a chain that begins in the character's core self, and dialogue problems are almost always story problems in disguise.
Practical workflow tip: For each scene, fill in the Scene Worksheet (Phase 5) with gerund-named beats before drafting dialogue, then run the diagnostic checklist (Phase 6) after. This forces the Character and Story layers to do their work before words ever hit the page.
