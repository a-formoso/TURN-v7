# Sample films — draft for approval (2 October 2026)

> **Status: approved by the owner (2 October 2026) — all three, as written.** Beats and screenplay: written
> (`docs/samples/Glass Hours.md`, `docs/samples/Kelpfold - What the Sea Gave Back.md`, `docs/samples/Third Mainland.md`).
>
> **Originally drafted for the owner's approval.** Three original films to show on the Home dashboard until a new
> user has 3 films of their own (`docs/Dashboard Plan.md` §4), replacing the built-in *The Matrix* sample as the
> showcase. Each one follows `docs/Story Pipeline.md` up to the spine (seed → shape → logline → research →
> synopsis → world, cast and scenes with their value charges). Beats and screenplay come after approval.
> Charges use the app's scale (−3 … +3); every scene turns its value.
>
> **Why these three:** three formats and three frameworks, so a new user sees the range in one glance — a
> **Short Film** in **Three-Act Turns**, a 30-second **Commercial** in **Kishōtenketsu**, a **Music Video** in
> **Hero's Journey**. Each has a look that will read well as key art: furnace fire and glass, a yellow coat in a
> grey storm, a night bridge in neon.
>
> All names, brands, songs and artists below are invented. Facts in the research are general knowledge, to be
> checked against the web when the film is built in the app (the Fact pillar does that on the default engine).

## 1) Glass Hours — Short Film · Three-Act Turns · 16 scenes · ~15 min

**Logline.** When the cracked furnace of her family's Murano glassworks gives her one month to sell, the
workshop's last master must decide whether to teach the young deckhand who watches through her window everything
she never taught her own son.

**Premise (a question, never a message).** What would happen if the last master of a dying glass workshop could
save it only by giving away the one thing she has kept to herself — her craft?

**Controlling idea (provisional).** A craft survives *when it is given away rather than kept* — ironic: the
workshop lives on, and she stops being its maker.

**Centre of good.** Graziella's hands: she has never once dropped a gather someone else depended on.

**Setting.** Contemporary, a winter of *acqua alta* · one month · a single family furnace on the Fondamenta dei
Vetrai, Murano · conflict: personal (mother and son) and institutional (the market buying the island's furnaces).

**Three Pillars of Research (brief).**
- *Memory:* the dread of being the last one who knows how; the quiet hurt of a child who left the family trade.
- *Imagination:* 5 a.m., the furnace roar, the second bench nobody sits at, a cold espresso on the marver, the
  smell of hot iron; a boy's breath fogging the workshop window every evening.
- *Fact:* the tools and words — *fornace* (furnace), the gather, the *canna* (blowpipe), the marver, annealing in
  the lehr, *reticello* (the net pattern where every glass thread crosses another). Energy costs closed or paused
  many Murano furnaces in 2022–23; Venetian buildings are being bought for hotels. Many workers on the vaporetto
  lines and in the city's service jobs come from abroad.

**Synopsis.**
- *The Setup.* At 72, Graziella Fiorin still lights the family furnace alone every dawn. Her son Luca arrives from
  Milan with an offer from a Scandinavian hotel group, which wants the building, and she refuses it. Every
  evening a young vaporetto deckhand, Samir Haddad, watches her work through the window; she chases him off. Then
  the furnace crown cracks, and the repair costs more than a year's sales: she has a month.
- *The Confrontation.* Samir comes back with cullet he gathered from the canal and asks to work for nothing.
  Graziella tests him, he burns his hand and doesn't drop the pipe, and she starts to teach him. Luca has
  already promised the buyer a chandelier for the hotel lobby as part of the sale; Graziella decides to make it
  with Samir, as her answer. Luca finds Samir at his late father's bench, and the old wound opens: she never let
  her own son touch the pipe. The hotel offers Samir a bellhop job with papers. Rushing, they ruin the anneal and
  the chandelier cracks in the lehr. Samir leaves.
- *The Resolution.* By the canal, Graziella tells Luca the truth: she kept him from the trade to spare him its
  poverty. He tells her he wanted it anyway. Luca goes to the hotel's staff door and brings Samir back. That
  night the three of them blow the piece together, and it holds. When the buyer comes for the keys, Graziella
  offers her the chandelier and a different deal: the hotel funds the furnace as a working studio, with an
  apprenticeship that gives Samir his papers. At dawn Samir works at the second bench, and Graziella watches.

## 2) Kelpfold — "What the Sea Gave Back" — Commercial · Kishōtenketsu · 4 scenes · 30 s

**The brand (invented).** **Kelpfold** makes waterproof rain shells from recovered "ghost nets", the fishing nets
lost at sea. **The promise (kept intact):** *fully waterproof shells made from nets pulled out of the sea.*

**Logline.** On a storm-dark morning in a northern fishing harbour, a girl in a yellow rain shell waits on the quay
for her grandfather's boat, and finds out what her coat used to be.

**Premise (inside the promise).** What would happen if a child learned that the thing keeping her dry was once a
danger to the sea her grandfather works?

**Controlling idea.** Care comes back *when we pull back what was lost* — idealistic.

**Setting.** Present day · one morning · a small fishing harbour in Lofoten, Norway · conflict: environmental
(the weather, the sea) — the human story is a warm one.

**Three Pillars (brief).**
- *Memory:* waiting for someone at the water's edge; the pride of wearing a grown-up's gift.
- *Imagination:* sideways rain, red fishing huts, gulls standing into the wind, a boat's lights through spray.
- *Fact:* lost and abandoned fishing gear is a large share of ocean plastic; old nylon nets can be recovered and
  recycled into new fabric. (The spot makes no number claims beyond the sleeve label, which is the brand's own.)

**Synopsis (four movements).**
- *Ki.* Rain blows sideways across the harbour; umbrellas turn inside out. Maren, nine, runs down the quay in a
  bright yellow Kelpfold shell.
- *Shō.* She waits at the end of the quay, dry, while the storm passes over her, until a small boat comes out of
  the spray. It sits low in the water.
- *Ten.* It isn't fish. Her grandfather Tor hauls a tangle of ghost net onto the quay. Maren touches the green
  fibres, then her own sleeve: the same green, and a label that reads "This shell was 2.4 kg of lost net."
- *Ketsu.* She opens her coat and pulls him in under it, both of them laughing in the rain. Super: **Kelpfold.
  Made from what the sea gave back.**

**Cast.**
| Name | Role | Want | Need | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MAREN | Protagonist — a fisherman's granddaughter, 9 | to be the first one at the quay when the boat comes in | to understand what her grandfather does out there | (no dialogue; laughter) |
| TOR | Mentor — her grandfather, a fisherman in his seventies who now hauls ghost nets | get the net ashore before the next squall | let himself be looked after | soft northern Norwegian accent, low and slow (one line, optional: "Look what I found.") |

**Scenes (≈ 7.5 s each).**

| 1 | Ki | Sideways Rain | EXT. HARBOUR STEPS · DAWN | Maren | Exposure −1 → Shelter +1 | Umbrellas fail; Maren runs down the steps, dry in her yellow shell. |
| 2 | Shō | The Quay's End | EXT. QUAY END · DAWN | Maren | Anticipation +1 → Worry −1 | She waits through the storm; the boat appears low and late. |

## 3) Third Mainland — Music Video · Hero's Journey · 8 scenes · ~4 min

**The song (invented).** "Third Mainland" by **Adaeze Rae**: alt-soul, ~4 minutes. The sections are Intro,
Verse 1, Pre-chorus, Chorus, Verse 2, Chorus, Bridge and Final chorus, one scene each. The music video follows the
song's sections; the framework orders the turns.

**Logline.** On the night of their mother's burial, a young tailor carries the family sewing machine on the last
bus across Lagos's longest bridge to the sister who blames her for staying behind.

**Premise.** What would happen if the only way to bring two estranged sisters back together was the machine their
mother sewed on — carried across a city that never stops moving?

**Controlling idea.** Family is rejoined *when we carry what we were left, not what we're owed* — idealistic.

**Setting.** Present day · one night, 10 p.m. to dawn · Lagos: a tailoring kiosk in Yaba, a yellow *danfo* bus,
the Third Mainland Bridge, a nurses' quarters on Lagos Island · conflict: personal (the sisters) and environmental
(the city at night: traffic, rain, a power cut).

**Three Pillars (brief).**
- *Memory:* the chores a parent leaves behind; the sibling who left and the sibling who stayed.
- *Imagination:* the kiosk's single bulb, bolts of Ankara print, the machine's treadle under a dust sheet; the
  conductor calling the route; the lagoon black under the bridge, Island lights ahead.
- *Fact:* yellow *danfo* minibuses run Lagos's routes; the Third Mainland Bridge crosses the lagoon from the
  mainland to Lagos Island; tailoring kiosks and *aso ebi* (matching family cloth for ceremonies) are part of
  family occasions.

**Synopsis (Departure · Initiation · Return).**
- *Departure.* After the burial, Temi sits in their mother's empty kiosk with the old treadle machine. A voice note
  from her sister Folake, a nurse on the Island: "Bring it, or don't come." Temi straps the machine to a trolley
  and boards the last danfo, where the old driver, Baba Sule, knew their mother's shop.
- *Initiation.* On the bridge, traffic stops dead and then the rain comes. A passenger's market bag tears and Temi
  mends it by phone light on the machine's case, so the bus starts to help her. At the Island end, the trolley
  hits a kerb, the machine falls and the treadle belt snaps.
- *Return.* Temi carries the machine the last streets in her arms and knocks at Folake's gate at 4 a.m. Folake
  finds the belt in Temi's pocket. At dawn the sisters re-thread it together, and the machine runs. On the
  bridge behind them, the morning traffic begins.

## What happens after you approve them

1. **Beats and screenplay** for each, through the pipeline (beat maps, then screenplay with Voice DNA), checked by
   the Story Editors.
2. **Their look** in the Art Room: Lookbook, cast and location sheets, a few key shots and a poster for each card.
   These are image generations on the admin account (roughly a few hundred credits per film).
3. **Shipping them as samples:** stored as read-only sample films with a **Make a copy** button, shown on Home
   until a user has 3 films of their own, then in Templates (`docs/Dashboard Plan.md` §4).
4. **The Matrix sample** stays only where it's used today for demos and tests, never on the dashboard.
