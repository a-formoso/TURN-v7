# Kelpfold — What the Sea Gave Back — beats and script

Commercial · Kishōtenketsu · 4 scenes · 30 s · original sample film for Kinolot (approved 2 October 2026). The brand is invented.

**The promise (kept intact):** fully waterproof shells made from nets pulled out of the sea. The spot *shows* the
waterproofing (rain that never gets through) and *tells* the origin only through the sleeve label and the end line.

## Changes from the approved spine

- **Value axes named.** Each scene now names one axis, "Positive pole ↔ Negative pole", and opens and closes on it.
  Scenes 1–3 use the approved words as their poles: *Shelter ↔ Exposure*, *Anticipation ↔ Worry*,
  *Recognition ↔ Mystery*.
- **Scene 4 only:** its approved words (Recognition +2 → Belonging +3) sat on two different axes. Its axis is now
  *Belonging ↔ Separation*, and the opening +2 reads "Recognition" as a point on it: she knows what he does out
  there, but he is still out in the rain alone. The numbers are unchanged.
- Nothing else changed: same events, order, drivers and charges.

## Scene 1 — Sideways Rain  (Ki) · ~7.5 s

- **Slugline:** EXT. HARBOUR STEPS · DAWN
- **Driver:** Maren
- **Reactor:** the storm (and the grown-ups caught in it)
- **Value at stake:** Shelter ↔ Exposure
- **Open → close:** Exposure −1 → Shelter +1
- **Conflict source:** extra-personal (the weather)
- **Conflict mode:** minimal
- **Structural role:** Ki — introduction: the world, the weather, the girl, the yellow shell. It only needs movement;
  it reverses anyway.

**Desire:** to reach the quay before the boat comes in.
**Obstacle:** the storm, to drive everyone off the harbour and indoors.

**Beats**
1. Drive — dash: Maren bursts out of a red hut door into the storm, zipping the yellow shell up to her chin.
   React — overpower: a gust snaps the umbrellas of two grown-ups on the steps inside out and drives them back
   under the eaves.
2. Drive — race (turn): she runs straight down the open steps through the sideways rain; water sheets off the
   shell and her face stays dry under the hood.
   React — the storm loses its hold: she is the only one out in it, and it can't get to her.

**Script** — 0:00–0:07.5

```
[b1]
EXT. HARBOUR STEPS · DAWN

Blue-grey dawn over a Lofoten harbour. Rain blows sideways
across a row of red fishing huts on stilts; black mountains
vanish into cloud. Wooden drying racks shudder in the wind.

A hut door bangs open. MAREN (9) bursts out in a bright yellow
rain shell, zipping it to her chin. A green inner cuff shows
as she tugs the sleeve over her fist.

On the steps below, two grown-ups in dark parkas fight their
umbrellas. A gust flips both inside out. They retreat under
the eaves, soaked.

[b2]
Maren runs past them, straight down the open steps, hood up.
Rain hammers the yellow fabric and runs off it in sheets.
Under the hood her face is dry. She doesn't slow down.

Long lens, low angle: the one yellow figure crossing a grey
frame, gulls hanging still in the wind above her.
```

## Scene 2 — The Quay's End  (Shō) · ~7.5 s

- **Slugline:** EXT. QUAY END · DAWN
- **Driver:** Maren
- **Reactor:** the sea (an empty horizon, then a late boat)
- **Value at stake:** Anticipation ↔ Worry
- **Open → close:** Anticipation +1 → Worry −1
- **Conflict source:** extra-personal (the sea and the weather)
- **Conflict mode:** minimal
- **Structural role:** Shō — development: the wait deepens; the boat's late, low arrival sets up the question the
  Ten answers.

**Desire:** to be the first one there when the boat comes in.
**Obstacle:** the sea, to keep the horizon empty and the boat away.

**Beats**
1. Drive — hold the post: Maren plants herself against the last bollard at the end of the quay; a wave bursts
   over the edge and the spray beads and rolls off her shell.
   React — withhold: the horizon gives back nothing but grey water; the gulls stand into the wind, waiting with
   her.
2. Drive — reach: she climbs onto the bollard and shades her eyes against the rain to see further.
   React — answer late (turn): two small boat lights come out of the spray, slow, the hull sitting low in the
   water. Her smile goes.

**Script** — 0:07.5–0:15

```
[b1]
EXT. QUAY END · DAWN

The end of a wooden quay, dark with rain. Maren stands against
the last iron bollard, rope coiled at her feet. A wave bursts
over the edge. Spray hits the yellow shell, beads and rolls
off. She doesn't flinch. A small white label tab at her cuff
flutters in the wind.

Beyond her: only grey sea and grey sky. Three gulls stand on
the quay rail, beaks into the wind, waiting with her.

[b2]
She climbs onto the bollard, one hand over her eyes.

Through the spray: two weak lights. A small fishing boat,
green hull, comes on slowly, sitting low in the water, as if
too heavy.

Close on Maren. The smile goes.
```

## Scene 3 — The Net  (Ten) · ~7.5 s

- **Slugline:** EXT. QUAY · DAWN
- **Driver:** Tor
- **Reactor:** the sea and the wind (the net that fights to go back), and Maren
- **Value at stake:** Recognition ↔ Mystery
- **Open → close:** Mystery 0 → Recognition +2
- **Conflict source:** extra-personal (the sea; the mystery of the net)
- **Conflict mode:** minimal
- **Structural role:** Ten — the twist: the cargo isn't fish, and her coat was once this. Reverses decisively from 0.

**Desire:** to get the net ashore before the next squall.
**Obstacle:** the sea and the wind, to drag the net back over the side.

**Beats**
1. Drive — heave: TOR braces in the stern and hauls a dripping tangle of old green net over the gunwale onto the
   quay.
   React — resist: the wind drags at it and it slaps back against the hull; Maren stares — no fish, just rope and
   weed.
2. Drive — offer: Tor kneels on the net to pin it and holds a strand of it out to her ("Look what I found." —
   optional line).
   React — match (turn): Maren rubs the green fibre between her fingers, then looks at her own sleeve. The same
   green.
3. React — read: she turns her cuff over and reads the label. INSERT of the label.

**Script** — 0:15–0:22.5

```
[b1]
EXT. QUAY · DAWN

The boat bumps the quay. TOR (70s), grey beard, orange oilskins
streaming, braces in the stern and hauls. A heavy tangle of old
fishing net comes over the gunwale: green fibres, knotted rope,
kelp, a cracked float. It slaps onto the wet boards.

The wind drags a corner back toward the water. Tor drops a knee
on it to pin it.

Maren looks into the boat. No fish. Only net.

[b2]
Tor pulls one strand loose and holds it out to her.

                    TOR
          (low, warm; optional)
     Look what I found.

Maren takes it. Rubs the green fibre between her fingers. Then
looks at her sleeve: the inner cuff, the stitching, the same
green.

[b3]
She turns the cuff over.

INSERT — THE SLEEVE LABEL, rain beading on it:
"This shell was 2.4 kg of lost net."

Maren looks up at Tor.
```

## Scene 4 — Under One Coat  (Ketsu) · ~7.5 s

- **Slugline:** EXT. QUAY · MORNING
- **Driver:** Maren
- **Reactor:** Tor (who doesn't let himself be looked after), and the next squall
- **Value at stake:** Belonging ↔ Separation
- **Open → close:** +2 (Recognition: she knows what he does, but he is still alone in the rain) → Belonging +3
- **Conflict source:** personal (his habit of looking after everyone but himself), against the weather
- **Conflict mode:** minimal
- **Structural role:** Ketsu — reconciliation: the twist is answered with care; the brand line lands. May settle.

**Desire:** to shelter her grandfather the way the coat shelters her.
**Obstacle:** Tor, to wave her off and keep working in the rain.

**Beats**
1. Drive — open up: the next squall hits; Maren unzips her yellow shell and holds it wide toward him.
   React — refuse: Tor shakes his head, wet to the skin, and waves her back to keep herself dry.
2. Drive — insist (turn): she steps up onto the net crate, now as tall as him, and pulls the open shell over his
   head and shoulders.
   React — give in: he laughs, ducks in under it, and they stand pressed together in the rain under one small
   yellow coat, both laughing.
3. The end line: SUPER and logo over the wide.

**Script** — 0:22.5–0:30

```
[b1]
EXT. QUAY · MORNING

A new squall comes across the harbour; the light lifts a
little behind it, silver on the water. Rain drives across the
quay. Tor, soaked, bends back to the net.

Maren unzips her shell and holds it open toward him, arms wide.

Tor shakes his head and waves her back, keep yourself dry.

[b2]
She steps up onto a fish crate, eye to eye with him, and pulls
the open shell up over his head and shoulders. It barely
covers them.

Tor laughs. He ducks in under it. The two of them stand pressed
together under one small yellow coat, rain bouncing off it,
both laughing.

[b3]
WIDE: the yellow shape at the end of the grey quay, the green
net at their feet, the red huts behind, gulls lifting off the
rail.

SUPER: Kelpfold. Made from what the sea gave back.

The Kelpfold logo holds to the end.
```

## Sound

- **Bed:** the storm is the score for the first half: wind in the hut eaves, rain drumming on fabric (close, crisp on
  the yellow shell, dull on the grown-ups' parkas), gulls crying into the wind, halyards knocking.
- **Scene 2:** the sea takes over: a wave bursting on the quay, then near-silence except the wind. The boat's engine
  arrives low and labouring, under the rain, before we see it.
- **Scene 3:** wet, heavy net dragged over wood; rope creaking; the float clacking on the boards. Tor's line, if
  used, is close and quiet, under the weather.
- **The motif:** a single warm instrument (a solo hardanger-style fiddle or a plain acoustic guitar, an original
  phrase) enters only at the label, on one held note.
- **Scene 4:** the motif opens into its short phrase as she pulls him under the coat. Their laughter, unscored, sits
  on top of the mix. The weather drops back under it for the super, and the phrase resolves on the logo.
- No voice-over. The super and the label are read, not spoken.
