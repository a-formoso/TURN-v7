# Third Mainland — beats and script

Music Video · Hero's Journey · 8 scenes · ~4 min · original sample film for Kinolot (approved 2 October 2026). Song and artist invented.

**The song (invented):** "Third Mainland" by **Adaeze Rae**, alt-soul, ~4 minutes. One scene per section, about
30 seconds each: Intro, Verse 1, Pre-chorus, Chorus, Verse 2, Chorus, Bridge, Final chorus. The video follows the
song's sections; the Hero's Journey orders the turns. The story is told in image and action. The only words are
Folake's voice note, a few from Baba Sule and the market woman, and one line between the sisters near the end.

## Changes from the approved spine

- **Value axes named.** Each scene now names one axis, "Positive pole ↔ Negative pole", and opens and closes on it.
  Scenes 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 use the approved words as their poles: *Duty ↔ Grief*, *Accompanied ↔ Alone*,
  *Momentum ↔ Fear*, *Kinship ↔ Stranger*, *Kinship ↔ Loss*.
- **Scene 2:** its approved words (Duty +1 → Rejection −2) sat on two different axes. Its axis is now
  *Belonging ↔ Rejection*. The opening +1 reads "Duty" as a point on it: keeping the kiosk is how Temi belongs.
- **Scene 7:** approved Loss −3 → Opening +1. Its axis is now *Opening ↔ Shut out*. The −3 it carries from
  Scene 6 is, at the gate, the loss of being shut out.
- **Scene 8:** approved Opening +1 → Reunion +3. Its axis is *Reunion ↔ Estrangement*, and +1 → +3 is a
  progression on the positive side. That is allowed here because the Return with the Elixir may settle.
- Nothing else changed: same events, order, drivers and charges. The beats add only small props and people inside
  the approved events: Folake's uncut aso ebi, their mother's hummed tune, a photo, a conductor and a market woman.

## Scene 1 — The Empty Kiosk  (Intro · Ordinary World) · ~0:30

- **Slugline:** INT. TAILORING KIOSK, YABA · NIGHT
- **Driver:** Temi
- **Reactor:** the empty kiosk (grief)
- **Value at stake:** Duty ↔ Grief
- **Open → close:** Grief −2 → Duty +1
- **Conflict source:** inner
- **Conflict mode:** reflexive
- **Timecode:** 0:00–0:30

**Desire:** to put her mother's kiosk to rights on the night of the burial.
**Obstacle:** grief, to stop her hands at every trace her mother left.

**Beats**
1. Drive — busywork: still in her burial aso ebi, Temi sweeps offcuts and squares the Ankara bolts, keeping her
   back to the sheeted machine.
   React — the kiosk answers in her mother's voice: the tape measure on its nail, the chalk worn to her mother's grip.
2. Drive — keep going: she reaches for the top shelf to tidy it.
   React — a folded length of the same wine-red cloth falls into her hands, with a paper tag in her mother's writing:
   *Folake*. Never sewn. Temi sits down on the floor with it.
3. Drive — face it (turn): she stands, pulls the dust sheet off the treadle machine, sits and sets her foot on the
   treadle. The belt bites and the wheel turns.
   React — her nervous hum becomes her mother's working tune; she threads the needle. The shop is hers to keep.

**Script** — 0:00–0:30

```
[b1]
INT. TAILORING KIOSK, YABA — NIGHT

A plywood kiosk painted sky-blue, zinc roof ticking as it
cools. One bare bulb on a twisted flex. Bolts of Ankara print
stacked to the rafters: indigo, saffron, leaf-green. A
dressmaker's torso stuck with pins.

In the doorway, a printed burial poster: CELEBRATION OF LIFE.
A smiling woman in a tall gele. Over the door, a hand-painted
sign: IYA FOLAKE FASHION · ASO EBI · ALTERATIONS.

TEMI OGUNLEYE (22), still in her burial aso ebi (wine-red and
gold, her gele loose on her shoulders), sweeps offcuts into a
pile. Fast, tidy, not looking at the corner.

In the corner, a shape under a white dust sheet: an old treadle
sewing machine. Its cast-iron stand shows at the hem.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing, hummed)
          Hmm-mm, hmm-mm...

A tape measure hangs on its nail. A stub of tailor's chalk is
worn into a groove the shape of a thumb. Temi's broom stops.

[b2]
She reaches up to straighten the top shelf. A folded length of
the same wine-red cloth slides into her hands. A paper tag is
pinned to it, in a careful old hand: "Folake."

Cut, never sewn. Temi slides down the wall to the floor,
holding it.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          One bulb burning, the cloth laid down,
          the wheel still waiting for a foot to come round.

[b3]
She gets up. She pulls off the dust sheet: black enamel, gold
scrollwork worn to brass, a leather belt running from the iron
treadle wheel up to the hand wheel. An old brass staple joins
the belt.

She sits. Bare foot on the treadle. Rocks it once, twice. The
belt bites. The wheel turns. The needle bar runs empty.

Temi hums under the bulb, her mother's tune now, steady. She
licks a thread and pushes it through the needle's eye.
```

## Scene 2 — The Voice Note  (Verse 1 · Call to Adventure) · ~0:30

- **Slugline:** INT. KIOSK · NIGHT
- **Driver:** Folake
- **Reactor:** Temi
- **Value at stake:** Belonging ↔ Rejection
- **Open → close:** Belonging (Duty) +1 → Rejection −2
- **Conflict source:** personal
- **Conflict mode:** asymmetric (Folake speaks from across the water; Temi can only answer by action)
- **Timecode:** 0:30–1:00

**Desire:** to claim their mother's machine without taking her sister back.
**Obstacle:** Temi, to be answered as a sister and not given orders.

**Beats**
1. Drive — demand: a voice note lands from Folake, five words and nothing else.
   React — Temi's foot freezes on the treadle. She calls back; it rings out, then "Declined".
2. Drive — close the door (turn): Folake sends only a map pin, a gate on Lagos Island. There is no greeting and no
   name, and no word for Temi herself.
   React — Temi records a reply and the unsayable breaks out: "I stayed—". She deletes it. Then she drags the
   market trolley out from under the counter and ropes the machine to it.

**Script** — 0:30–1:00

```
[b1]
INT. KIOSK — NIGHT

The needle runs a test seam across a scrap of indigo. Temi's
phone buzzes on the machine's wooden table. The screen reads
"Folake (Sister)" over a voice-note waveform, 0:02.

She presses play.

                    FOLAKE (V.O.)
                    (on the voice note; crisp, tired)
          Bring it, or don't come.

The treadle stops dead. The needle stops in the cloth.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          Five small words from across the water,
          sharp as the pins in a Sunday collar.

Temi calls. It rings out under the bulb. CALL DECLINED.

[b2]
A new message: a dropped map pin. NURSES' QUARTERS · GATE 2 ·
LAGOS ISLAND. Nothing else.

Temi holds the record button. Her mouth opens.

                    TEMI
                    (into the phone)
          I stayed—

She lets go. The waveform hangs on the screen. She deletes it.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          I kept the doors and you kept the distance.

She hauls a two-wheeled market trolley out from under the
counter and ropes the machine onto it: stand, wheel, head,
belt. She closes the wooden case over the head and slides
Folake's wine-red cloth inside it.

She pulls the bulb's chain. Dark. A padlock snaps shut on the
kiosk door.
```

## Scene 3 — Last Danfo  (Pre-chorus · Meeting the Mentor) · ~0:30

- **Slugline:** EXT. YABA BUS STOP · NIGHT
- **Driver:** Baba Sule
- **Reactor:** Temi (and the city's last buses)
- **Value at stake:** Accompanied ↔ Alone
- **Open → close:** Alone −2 → Accompanied +1
- **Conflict source:** extra-personal (the city at night: full buses, no room for load)
- **Conflict mode:** indirect (his teasing hides a kindness)
- **Timecode:** 1:00–1:30

**Desire:** to finish the last route on time.
**Obstacle:** a girl with an iron machine and no other way across, to hold up his bus.

**Beats**
1. Drive — hurry on: the last yellow danfo slows only to let its conductor shout the route. The conductor sees the
   trolley and waves her off, and the bus rolls on.
   React — Temi runs after it, the trolley clattering over the broken kerbs, one hand up.
2. Drive — tease (turn): brake lights. Baba Sule sees the machine in his wing mirror and knows it; he makes her pay
   for the machine like a passenger. Then the unsayable comes out: he wore this machine's work for years.
   React — Temi laughs for the first time tonight and pays. The conductor and two passengers haul the machine
   aboard. She is not carrying it alone.

**Script** — 1:00–1:30

```
[b1]
EXT. YABA BUS STOP — NIGHT

A roadside stop under a dead streetlight, lit by the orange
glow of a roasted-corn seller's coals. Temi stands alone with
the roped machine. Buses pass, packed, with arms out of the
windows.

Headlights. The last yellow DANFO of the night, black stripes
down its side, dented, its sliding door open. A young
CONDUCTOR (17) hangs out of the door.

                    CONDUCTOR
          Obalende! Obalende last!

He sees the trolley and slaps the bus's side.

                    CONDUCTOR (CONT'D)
          No load! No space!

The danfo rolls on. Temi runs after it, the trolley jolting
over potholes, one hand up.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          Last bus calling, the city won't wait,

[b2]
Brake lights, red on the wet tar. In the wing mirror: the
iron treadle, the gold scrollwork.

BABA SULE (60s), grey cap, prayer beads looped on the gearstick,
leans out of the driver's window.

                    BABA SULE
          Iya Folake's machine.
                    (a long look at her)
          It pays fare like a person.

Temi digs out crumpled naira notes. He doesn't take them yet.

                    BABA SULE (CONT'D)
                    (quiet)
          Every Sallah, she hemmed my agbada on that.

He takes the money. Temi laughs, wet-eyed. The conductor and
two passengers lift the machine through the sliding door.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          an old man laughing at an iron weight,
          lift it, lift it, there's room.
```

## Scene 4 — Onto the Bridge  (Chorus · Crossing the Threshold) · ~0:30

- **Slugline:** EXT. THIRD MAINLAND BRIDGE · NIGHT (and INT. DANFO)
- **Driver:** Temi
- **Reactor:** the bridge (the lagoon, the distance, the sister at the far end)
- **Value at stake:** Momentum ↔ Fear
- **Open → close:** Fear −1 → Momentum +2
- **Conflict source:** inner (and extra-personal: the water, the dark)
- **Conflict mode:** reflexive
- **Timecode:** 1:30–2:00

**Desire:** to hold the machine, and herself, together across the water.
**Obstacle:** fear of the dark lagoon and of who waits at the far end, which wants her to turn back.

**Beats**
1. Drive — brace: Temi wedges the trolley with her knees and wraps both arms round the machine as the danfo climbs
   onto the bridge.
   React — the bridge opens out: black water on both sides, the wind slamming through the open windows, the Island's
   lights very far away. She shuts her eyes.
2. Drive — keep time (turn): she slips her bare foot onto the treadle and rocks it in time with the engine. The
   wheel spins and the needle bar dances in its case.
   React — Baba Sule drums the steering wheel to her rhythm and the conductor slaps the side to it. She opens her
   eyes and looks straight ahead at the lights.

**Script** — 1:30–2:00

```
[b1]
INT. DANFO — NIGHT — MOVING

The bus climbs the ramp. Temi sits on the floor by the sliding
door, knees locked round the trolley, both arms round the
machine's wooden case. Eyes shut.

EXT. THIRD MAINLAND BRIDGE — NIGHT

The danfo, small and yellow, runs onto the long low bridge.
Sodium lamps in a line that never ends. Below, the lagoon is
black. Far off, a scatter of lamps from stilt houses on the
water. Ahead, the Island's towers are pinpricks.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          Carry it over, Third Mainland,
          black water under, the lights on the other hand,

[b2]
INT. DANFO — NIGHT — MOVING

Temi's bare foot finds the treadle. She rocks it to the
engine's beat. The iron wheel spins and the needle bar
chatters inside the case.

Up front, Baba Sule's fingers drum the wheel in the same time.
The conductor, hanging from the door, slaps the roof to it.

Temi opens her eyes. She looks straight ahead.

EXT. DANFO ROOF, THIRD MAINLAND BRIDGE — NIGHT (PERFORMANCE)

ADAEZE RAE stands on the moving roof among the bound luggage,
in a long coat patched from a hundred Ankara offcuts, gold
hoops, her head shaved close. The bridge lamps strobe over her.
A river of tail lights runs ahead toward the Island.

                    ADAEZE RAE
                    (singing)
          foot on the treadle, the engine in time,
          carry it over, it's yours, it was mine.
```

## Scene 5 — Phone Light  (Verse 2 · Tests, Allies) · ~0:30

- **Slugline:** INT. DANFO (STOPPED) · NIGHT · RAIN
- **Driver:** Temi
- **Reactor:** the passengers (a MARKET WOMAN, 50s, in a wrapper and headtie)
- **Value at stake:** Kinship ↔ Stranger
- **Open → close:** Stranger −1 → Kinship +2
- **Conflict source:** extra-personal (the stalled bridge, the rain, a crowded bus that resents her load)
- **Conflict mode:** minimal
- **Timecode:** 2:00–2:30

**Desire:** to earn her place on a stalled bus.
**Obstacle:** the bus, to treat her and her iron load as a nuisance in the rain.

**Beats**
1. Drive — make amends: when the market woman's bag splits on the trolley's buckle, Temi gets down on the wet floor
   and gathers the rolling tomatoes and peppers.
   React — the woman snatches the bag back and blames the machine. The passengers glare and the split runs wider.
2. Drive — offer the craft: Temi opens the machine's case, lays the torn bag across it and threads the needle by
   phone light.
   React — the woman folds her arms. Temi needs both hands to feed the seam, and the phone slides off the case into
   darkness.
3. Drive — ask (turn): into the dark she says one word, "Light?"
   React — one by one the passengers' phones come on round her. The woman holds the bag taut while Temi treadles a
   doubled lockstitch along the split, then bites off the thread and thanks her. The bus is helping her now.

**Script** — 2:00–2:30

```
[b1]
INT. DANFO — NIGHT — STOPPED

Dead still. The engine is off and the cabin lights are out to
save the battery. Rain hammers the roof and sheets down the
windows. Outside, a frozen line of red tail lights runs to the
horizon.

The MARKET WOMAN (50s), in an adire wrapper and a tight
headtie, edges past the trolley. Her woven nylon market bag
snags on its buckle and splits open. Tomatoes and scotch
bonnets roll down the aisle.

Temi drops to the wet floor, gathering them in her skirt.

                    MARKET WOMAN
                    (snatching the bag)
          This your iron!

Faces in the dark, glaring. The split gapes wider.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          Rain on the roof and the engines asleep,
          a stranger's bag spilling more than it keeps,

[b2]
Temi unbuckles the machine's wooden case and folds it open.
The black head gleams. She takes the bag and spreads it across
the case. She props her phone and threads the needle by its
light.

The woman watches, arms folded. Temi needs both hands to guide
the seam. The phone slides off the case and its light goes
face-down into the dark.

[b3]
Darkness. Rain. Temi holds the needle up where her phone was.

                    TEMI
          Light?

A beat. One phone comes on behind her. Then another, then ten:
small white lights in a ring round the machine, rain running
silver on the windows behind them.

Temi treadles. Close: the needle runs a doubled lockstitch
along the split, forward, then back over itself. The market
woman holds the bag taut with both fists.

She bites off the thread. Tests the seam. Holds.

                    MARKET WOMAN
                    (softly)
          Ẹ ṣé, ọmọ mi.

Her thank-you, "my child", passes along the bus as a murmur.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          ten small lights in a stalled line of cars,
          a needle can find you wherever you are.
```

## Scene 6 — The Kerb  (Chorus · The Ordeal) · ~0:30

- **Slugline:** EXT. LAGOS ISLAND STREET · NIGHT
- **Driver:** Temi
- **Reactor:** the city at night (a power cut, the dark streets)
- **Value at stake:** Kinship ↔ Loss
- **Open → close:** Kinship +2 → Loss −3
- **Conflict source:** extra-personal
- **Conflict mode:** minimal
- **Timecode:** 2:30–3:00

**Desire:** to cover the last streets to her sister's gate before dawn.
**Obstacle:** the dark Island, to take the machine from her.

**Beats**
1. Drive — let them go: at the Island end the whole bus passes the machine out of the door hand to hand. Temi waves
   the danfo on so it can finish its route.
   React — Baba Sule blesses her, then the danfo pulls away with Adaeze Rae singing on its roof. Its tail lights
   shrink, and Temi is alone at the kerbside.
2. Drive — run for it: a power cut has blacked out the street, so she pushes the trolley at a run through the
   puddles, following the map pin's blue dot.
   React — the dark hides the street: a generator's drone, a gutter and a high kerb she can't see.
3. Drive — force it over (turn): she shoves the trolley at the kerb. A wheel shears off, the machine pitches onto
   the wet road and the belt snaps with a crack. The song cuts to silence.
   React — on her knees, the treadle wheel still spinning free, she coils the broken belt and pushes it into her
   pocket.

**Script** — 2:30–3:00

```
[b1]
EXT. LAGOS ISLAND, BUS STOP — NIGHT

The rain has stopped. Wet tar shines. The danfo's sliding door
opens and the machine comes out hand to hand: the conductor,
two passengers, the market woman with her mended bag on her
shoulder, then down to the kerb.

Baba Sule leans out.

                    BABA SULE
          Go well, ọmọ Iya Folake.

She waves him on. The danfo pulls away.

EXT. DANFO ROOF — NIGHT (PERFORMANCE)

Adaeze Rae on the roof as it goes, singing back toward the
small figure at the kerb.

                    ADAEZE RAE
                    (singing)
          Carry it over, Third Mainland,

Its tail lights shrink. Temi stands alone.

[b2]
EXT. LAGOS ISLAND STREET — NIGHT

Power cut. Every streetlight is dead. A generator hums behind
a high wall. One kerosene lamp on a closed kiosk. Temi runs the
trolley through puddles, phone in her teeth, the map pin's blue
dot shaking on its screen.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          the road gives out where the dark streets stand,

[b3]
A high kerb, invisible in the black. The trolley hits it.

A wheel shears off. The machine pitches and slams onto the
road. The belt cracks like a whip.

SILENCE. The song cuts out.

The treadle wheel spins free in the dark, ticking, slowing.
Temi kneels on the wet tar. She picks up the snapped belt,
brass staple and all, coils it tight and pushes it deep into
her pocket.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing, alone, almost spoken)
          what's broken is still yours to hold.
```

## Scene 7 — Four A.M.  (Bridge · The Road Back) · ~0:30

- **Slugline:** EXT. NURSES' QUARTERS GATE · NIGHT
- **Driver:** Folake
- **Reactor:** Temi
- **Value at stake:** Opening ↔ Shut out
- **Open → close:** Shut out −3 → Opening +1
- **Conflict source:** personal
- **Conflict mode:** asymmetric (Folake holds the gate; Temi fights with her sister's own words)
- **Timecode:** 3:00–3:30

**Desire:** to keep the gate between them and need nothing from home.
**Obstacle:** Temi, bleeding at the gate with the broken machine in her arms, to be let in.

**Beats**
1. Drive — keep the gate shut: Folake comes to the bars in her scrubs, looks at the broken machine and not at her
   sister, and leaves the lock where it is.
   React — Temi holds her phone to the bars and plays the voice note back: "Bring it, or don't come." She brought it.
2. Drive — treat, don't greet: Folake sees the blood on Temi's palms. The nurse in her unlocks the gate, kneels and
   cleans the rope burns, eyes down, clinical.
   React — Temi keeps her right fist buried in her pocket and won't give it up.
3. Drive — open the fist (turn): Folake takes the wrist and draws the hand out. The coiled belt comes with it,
   brass staple and all. Then the unsayable comes out: "I left."
   React — Temi says nothing. She bends to one side of the machine and Folake takes the other. They carry it in
   through the gate.

**Script** — 3:00–3:30

```
[b1]
EXT. NURSES' QUARTERS GATE — NIGHT

A long walled street before dawn. Temi comes up it with the
machine in her arms, hugging the iron stand to her chest,
stopping every few houses to set it down and breathe. Rope
burns on both palms.

A green iron gate, painted many times. GATE 2. A bell push.
She rings. Her phone reads 4:02.

FOLAKE OGUNLEYE (31), navy scrubs, a cardigan over them,
hair wrapped for sleep, comes to the bars. She looks at the
machine on the ground: the missing belt, the dented case. Not
at her sister. Her hand stays off the bolt.

Temi holds her phone up to the bars. Plays it.

                    FOLAKE (V.O.)
                    (on the voice note)
          Bring it, or don't come.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          Four in the morning, iron and bell,

[b2]
Folake's eyes drop to Temi's hands: raw, bleeding. The bolt
scrapes back. She kneels on the step, pulls gauze and a
sachet of antiseptic from her scrubs pocket and cleans one
palm. Brisk. Eyes down.

She reaches for the other hand. Temi keeps it buried in her
pocket, a fist.

[b3]
Folake takes the wrist and draws the hand out. The fist opens.
The coiled belt, brass staple and all, unrolls across her
knee.

Folake holds it. A long moment.

                    FOLAKE
                    (barely)
          I left.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          I brought you the pieces, I brought you myself.

Temi says nothing. She bends and takes one side of the
machine. Folake takes the other. They lift it through the gate
together.
```

## Scene 8 — Re-threading  (Final chorus · Return with the Elixir) · ~0:30

- **Slugline:** INT. FOLAKE'S ROOM · DAWN
- **Driver:** Temi
- **Reactor:** Folake
- **Value at stake:** Reunion ↔ Estrangement
- **Open → close:** Reunion (an opening) +1 → Reunion +3 (a progression: the Elixir settles)
- **Conflict source:** inner (Temi's guilt at being the one who stayed) and personal
- **Conflict mode:** minimal
- **Timecode:** 3:30–4:00

**Desire:** to make their mother's machine run again, with her sister.
**Obstacle:** her bandaged hands and the years between them, to keep each sister working alone.

**Beats**
1. Drive — mend it herself: Temi sets the machine up by the window and starts to stitch the snapped belt back
   together.
   React — her bandaged fingers can't pull the heavy thread tight. She stops.
2. Drive — hand it over: without a word, Temi holds out the belt's loose end to Folake.
   React — Folake takes it and pulls the thread taut while Temi stitches: the same doubled lockstitch as on the bus,
   now with two pairs of hands.
3. Drive — run it (turn; the scene settles): they loop the joined belt back onto the wheels and Temi treadles. The
   machine runs, and she feeds Folake's wine-red cloth under the needle.
   React — Folake starts to hum their mother's tune first, and Temi joins her. Across the lagoon the morning
   traffic begins on the bridge.

**Script** — 3:30–4:00

```
[b1]
INT. FOLAKE'S ROOM — DAWN

A narrow room in the nurses' quarters: single bed, a ceiling
fan, two starched uniforms on a hook. Blue light at the
window. Below it, the lagoon, and the long line of the bridge.

Tucked in the mirror frame, an old, faded photo: two girls,
one small, one tall, under a hand-painted sign. IYA FOLAKE
FASHION. Folake kept it.

Temi rights the machine by the window. She lays the snapped
belt across her knee, pierces the leather with a heavy needle
and draws the thread through. She pulls. Her bandaged fingers
won't close on it. She stops.

[b2]
She holds the loose end out. She doesn't look up.

Folake, in the doorway with the first-aid roll, crosses and
takes it. She pulls the thread taut. Temi stitches.

Close, framed as on the bus: a doubled lockstitch, forward,
then back over itself. Four hands.

                    ADAEZE RAE (V.O.)
                    (singing)
          Carry it over, Third Mainland,
          two pairs of hands where there used to be one,

[b3]
They loop the joined belt through the table onto the treadle
wheel and the hand wheel. Temi sets her foot on the treadle.
Rocks it. The belt bites. The wheel turns. The needle runs.

Temi takes the wine-red cloth with Folake's paper tag and
feeds it under the needle. The first seam runs.

Folake begins to hum, low: their mother's tune. Temi looks up
and joins her.

EXT. THIRD MAINLAND BRIDGE — DAWN (PERFORMANCE)

Pink light on the lagoon. The morning traffic starts across
the bridge, and among it a dented yellow danfo. Adaeze Rae
sits on its roof, coat open to the wind, singing toward the
Island.

                    ADAEZE RAE
                    (singing)
          the wheel goes slow, then the wheel goes on,
          carry it over, carry it home.

INT. FOLAKE'S ROOM — DAWN

The two sisters at one machine, humming. The needle runs.

                                                   FADE OUT.
```
